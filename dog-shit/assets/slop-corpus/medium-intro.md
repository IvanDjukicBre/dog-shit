## A Brief History

To understand where we're going, we first need to understand where we've been.

In 1954, John Backus and his team at IBM began work on FORTRAN. It was the first widely used high-level programming language, and it changed everything. Suddenly, programmers no longer had to think in terms of machine instructions. They could think in terms of **problems**.

This was a paradigm shift.

Then came C. Then C++. Then Java. Then Python. Each language raised the level of abstraction. Each one let us think less about the machine and more about the problem.

Do you see the pattern?

Every major leap in software development has been a leap in **abstraction**. And artificial intelligence represents the next leap. Possibly the final one.

## The Three Pillars

In my experience working with dozens of engineering teams, I've identified three pillars of the AI transformation:

1. **Augmentation** — AI doesn't replace developers, it amplifies them
2. **Acceleration** — What took days now takes hours
3. **Accessibility** — Anyone can now build software

Let's explore each one.

### Pillar One: Augmentation

Here's the thing nobody tells you about AI: it's not about replacement. It's about **augmentation**.

Think of it like this. When calculators were invented, mathematicians didn't disappear. They just stopped doing arithmetic by hand and started solving harder problems.

The same thing is happening now.

### Pillar Two: Acceleration

Speed matters. In today's competitive landscape, the ability to ship quickly is often the difference between success and failure.

Consider the following statistics:

- 73% of developers report increased productivity
- 68% of teams ship features faster
- 91% say they would not go back

## What This Means For You
